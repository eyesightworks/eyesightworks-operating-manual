# 2. Executive Summary

The Vision Parking Lot is a controlled repository for ideas, opportunities, product concepts, technologies, business possibilities, and strategic directions that may be valuable to EyesightWorks but are **not currently active priorities**.

The purpose is to prevent good ideas from being lost while also preventing the company from constantly changing direction.

The Vision Parking Lot creates a deliberate separation between:

* what the company is doing now
* what the company may do later
* what the company has considered but should not pursue
* what requires more evidence before becoming a priority

The central principle is:

> **Capture the idea without committing the company to the idea.**

An idea entering the Vision Parking Lot is not automatically approved, funded, scheduled, or promised.

# 3. Purpose

This document establishes the framework for capturing and managing future ideas and opportunities.

It exists to help EyesightWorks:

* preserve valuable ideas
* reduce distraction
* avoid premature development
* separate vision from current execution
* record strategic possibilities
* revisit opportunities when conditions change
* prevent repeated discussions about the same idea
* maintain long-term strategic memory

* a place for future opportunities
* a controlled idea repository
* a source of potential future initiatives
* a protection against losing useful ideas
* a mechanism for separating ideas from commitments

---

# 6. What the Vision Parking Lot Is Not

The Vision Parking Lot is not:

* the active product roadmap
* a development backlog
* a task list
* a commitment to build
* an approval for spending
* a customer promise
* a substitute for validation
* a replacement for the Decision Log
* a replacement for the Risk Register

An idea should not be developed simply because it exists in this document.

---

# 7. Vision Parking Lot Philosophy

EyesightWorks recognizes that ideas will appear faster than the company can execute them.

This is normal.

The company therefore needs a way to capture ideas without allowing every idea to become a new project.

The operating principle is:

> **Capture → Preserve → Evaluate → Validate → Prioritize → Execute or Archive**

The existence of an idea does not create an obligation to act.

# 8. Core Principles

## 8.1 Capture Ideas Quickly

Potentially valuable ideas should be captured before they are forgotten.

---

## 8.2 Do Not Confuse Ideas With Priorities

An idea may be interesting without being important enough to execute now.

---

## 8.3 Evidence Increases Priority

Ideas supported by customer evidence, market evidence, business evidence, or measurable demand should receive more serious evaluation.

---

## 8.4 Current Priorities Come First

Active company commitments should generally take priority over newly discovered ideas unless there is a deliberate decision to change direction.

---

## 8.5 Parking Is Not Rejection

An idea may be parked simply because:

* timing is wrong
* resources are limited
* evidence is insufficient
* another priority is more important
* required technology is not ready
* the business model is unclear
* customer demand has not been validated

---

## 8.6 Revisit Based on Triggers

Ideas should be reconsidered when relevant evidence or conditions change.

---

## 8.7 Avoid Idea Accumulation Without Review

A parking lot should not become an unreviewed list of hundreds of forgotten ideas.

Periodic review is required.

# 11. Vision Parking Lot Entry Standard

Every significant entry should contain enough information to understand the idea later.

Recommended fields:

| Field          | Description                                 |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| ID             | Unique reference                            |
| Idea           | Name of opportunity                         |
| Category       | Product, business, technology, market, etc. |
| Problem        | Problem the idea may solve                  |
| Target User    | Intended customer or user                   |
| Opportunity    | Why it may matter                           |
| Evidence       | Existing evidence                           |
| Expected Value | Potential benefit                           |
| Dependencies   | What must exist first                       |
| Reason Parked  | Why it is not active now                    |
| Trigger        | What would justify review                   |
| Status         | Current parking status                      |
| Owner          | Person responsible for monitoring           |
| Date Added     | Date captured                               |
| Last Reviewed  | Most recent review                          |

# 13. Status Lifecycle

Vision Parking Lot entries should have a controlled status.

## Status 1 — Captured

The idea has been recorded.

No evaluation has yet occurred.

---

## Status 2 — Parked

The idea has been reviewed and intentionally kept outside the current execution priorities.

---

## Status 3 — Monitoring

The company is watching for evidence or market conditions that may change its relevance.

---

## Status 4 — Under Evaluation

The idea is being actively evaluated.

This may include:

* customer research
* market research
* technical analysis
* financial analysis
* experiments

---

## Status 5 — Validating

The idea has enough potential value to justify structured validation.

---

## Status 6 — Prioritized

The idea has been formally selected for potential execution.

A separate product or project process should then take ownership.

---

## Status 7 — Active

The idea has entered active execution.

The relevant roadmap, product requirements, project plan, or engineering process becomes the source of truth.

---

## Status 8 — Archived

The idea is no longer considered an active opportunity.

Archived ideas should remain available for historical reference.

---

# 15. Why Ideas Become Parked

An idea may be parked for several legitimate reasons.

Examples:

* insufficient evidence
* limited resources
* poor timing
* unclear customer demand
* unclear business model
* technical dependency
* operational complexity
* current strategic priorities are more important
* regulatory uncertainty
* market conditions are not yet favorable
* required infrastructure is unavailable
* customer problem is not sufficiently understood

Parking should represent a deliberate decision, not neglect.

# 16. Priority Separation

EyesightWorks should maintain a clear distinction between:

### Now

Work currently required to achieve active objectives.

### Next

Potential work that may follow current priorities.

### Later

Ideas that may become relevant after additional evidence or resources.

### Parking Lot

Ideas without sufficient priority or evidence for active planning.

```text id="g9a3kq"
NOW
 ↓
NEXT
 ↓
LATER
 ↓
VISION PARKING LOT
```

The Vision Parking Lot should not replace the active roadmap.

# 17. Evidence Requirements

An idea should generally move out of the parking lot only when there is stronger justification.

Evidence may include:

* customer interviews
* customer requests
* repeated problem reports
* usage data
* market research
* competitor analysis
* revenue evidence
* experiments
* technical validation
* operational evidence
* partnership interest

The evidence required should depend on the size and risk of the opportunity.

---

# 18. Customer Evidence

Customer problems should be validated before substantial product investment where practical.

Document 09 defines the Customer Feedback framework.

A parking-lot idea may therefore move toward validation when:

* the problem is repeatedly observed
* multiple target users identify the same issue
* customer demand becomes measurable
* customers demonstrate willingness to use or pay
* experiments produce useful evidence

Customer enthusiasm alone should not automatically create a development commitment.

---

# 19. Business Value Evaluation

Ideas moving toward active consideration should be evaluated using the Business Value framework in Document 12 where appropriate.

Relevant criteria may include:

* customer value
* revenue potential
* strategic alignment
* market opportunity
* evidence strength
* feasibility
* time to value

A high potential score does not automatically require execution.

It provides structured decision support.

---

# 20. Decision Governance

An idea should enter the Decision Log when it results in a significant strategic decision.

Examples:

* deciding to pursue the opportunity
* deciding not to pursue it
* changing company strategy because of it
* committing significant resources
* entering a major partnership
* changing architecture or infrastructure
* entering a new market

The Vision Parking Lot records the opportunity.

The Decision Log records the significant decision.

# 23. Technology Parking

Technology ideas should receive special discipline.

Examples may include:

* a new database
* a new framework
* a new AI model
* a new cloud platform
* a new infrastructure tool
* a new messaging system

The existence of a technology opportunity does not justify adoption.

* there is a clear problem
* the current solution is insufficient
* the new technology creates measurable value
* the added complexity is justified
* the company has sufficient capability to operate it

This supports the principle:

> **Problem First → Technology Second**

# 25. Vision Parking Lot and Innovation

Innovation is encouraged, but innovation must remain connected to company objectives.

A useful idea should answer:

* What problem does it solve?
* For whom?
* Why now?
* What evidence exists?
* What value might it create?
* What would be required?
* What would we have to stop or delay to pursue it?

The final question is especially important because company capacity is limited.

### Strategic Review

# 27. Parking Lot Review Process

A review should determine:

1. Is the idea still relevant?
2. Has new evidence appeared?
3. Has customer demand changed?
4. Has the market changed?
5. Has the technology changed?
6. Has the company's strategy changed?
7. Is the idea more valuable now?
8. Is the idea less valuable now?
9. Should it remain parked?
10. Should it move to validation?
11. Should it be prioritized?
12. Should it be archived?

# 29. Capacity Rule

EyesightWorks should recognize that every new initiative creates opportunity cost.

Before activating a major parking-lot idea, leadership should consider:

> **What existing priority will this affect?**

Possible outcomes include:

* continue current priorities
* replace an existing priority
* postpone an existing priority
* run a limited experiment
* reject the new opportunity

New ideas should not silently become additional commitments.

---

# 30. Idea Investment Levels

Parking-lot ideas may receive different levels of investment.

### Level 0 — Capture

Record the idea.

### Level 1 — Monitor

Watch for evidence.

### Level 2 — Research

Conduct limited research.

### Level 3 — Validate

Run a structured experiment or customer validation.

### Level 4 — Prototype

Build a limited proof of concept where justified.

### Level 5 — Product Investment

Commit meaningful resources.

The company should avoid jumping directly from Level 0 to Level 5 without appropriate evidence.

---

# 31. Opportunity Assessment Questions

Before promoting an idea toward active execution, ask:

### Customer

* Who has the problem?
* How frequently does it occur?
* How painful is it?
* How do they solve it today?

### Business

* How could the company create value?
* How could the company generate revenue?
* Does it support strategic objectives?

### Evidence

* What do we know?
* What do we only assume?
* What still needs validation?

### Technical

* Can we build it?
* What dependencies exist?
* What complexity will it introduce?

### Operational

* Can the company support it?
* What processes will be required?

### Strategic

* Why should EyesightWorks pursue it?
* Why now?
* What would have to be deprioritized?

# 32. Idea Promotion Criteria

A parking-lot idea may be promoted when:

* the problem is sufficiently understood
* customer evidence is credible
* business value is meaningful
* strategic alignment is clear
* feasibility is acceptable
* required resources are available
* risks are understood
* the company has a clear reason to act now

Promotion should be deliberate.

# 33. Idea Rejection and Archiving

Not every idea should survive indefinitely.

An idea may be archived when:

* evidence consistently indicates weak demand
* the opportunity is no longer relevant
* strategic conditions have changed
* the business model is not viable
* the opportunity has been superseded
* the idea conflicts with company direction
* the required investment is unjustified

Archiving is not failure.

It is a decision to stop allocating attention to an opportunity.

---

# 34. Reopening Archived Ideas

An archived idea may be reconsidered when meaningful new evidence appears.

A reopened idea should receive a new review rather than automatically returning to its former status.

Relevant new information should be recorded.

---

# 35. Avoiding Duplicate Ideas

Before adding a new entry, check whether a similar idea already exists.

Duplicate entries create:

* confusion
* repeated discussion
* fragmented evidence
* inconsistent decisions

Similar ideas should normally be linked or consolidated.

---

# 36. Naming Standards

Vision Parking Lot entries should use clear names.

Prefer:

> AI-Assisted Opportunity Matching

over:

> Cool AI Thing

Names should describe the opportunity rather than the excitement around it.

---

# 37. Ownership

Every significant parking-lot entry should have an owner where practical.

The owner is responsible for:

* maintaining the entry
* recording important evidence
* updating status
* identifying review triggers
* presenting the idea for evaluation when appropriate

Ownership does not mean the owner is automatically responsible for building the idea.

---

# 38. Governance Rules

The Vision Parking Lot follows these governance rules:

1. Ideas may be added without approval when clearly documented.
2. Parking an idea does not approve development.
3. Active company priorities remain protected.
4. Significant resource commitments require appropriate approval.
5. Important decisions should be recorded in the Decision Log.
6. Significant risks should be recorded in the Risk Register.
7. Active product requirements should live in the appropriate product documentation.
8. Ideas should be periodically reviewed.
9. Archived ideas should remain historically accessible.
10. The parking lot should remain manageable rather than becoming an unlimited backlog.

---

# 39. Vision Parking Lot Health Indicators

The Vision Parking Lot is healthy when:

* ideas are captured consistently
* active work is protected from unnecessary distraction
* important opportunities are not forgotten
* ideas have clear status
* important entries have owners
* evidence is attached where available
* outdated ideas are archived
* reviews lead to decisions
* the active roadmap remains focused

It may be unhealthy when:

* almost every idea becomes an active project
* ideas remain untouched for years without review
* the parking lot becomes extremely large
* teams repeatedly discuss the same ideas
* priorities constantly change because of new ideas
* ideas lack context or ownership
* the parking lot becomes an excuse to avoid making decisions

The Vision Parking Lot and Risk Register serve different purposes.

> What opportunities may be worth pursuing?

# 45. Relationship With Product Requirements

The Vision Parking Lot captures possibilities.

Product Requirements define what an active product must actually do.

Therefore:

> **Do not use the Vision Parking Lot as the active product specification.**

Once an idea is approved for active product development, the relevant product requirements document should contain the detailed requirements.
