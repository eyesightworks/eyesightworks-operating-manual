# Product Roadmap

## Executive Summary

The Product Roadmap defines how EyesightWorks Technologies moves validated opportunities from business problems to products, customer validation, commercialization, growth, platform integration, maturity, and eventual expansion, optimization, or retirement.

The roadmap is a strategic system rather than a fixed promise of dates.

Its purpose is to establish how the company identifies, evaluates, prioritizes, validates, builds, commercializes, and invests in product opportunities.

Product development should follow evidence rather than assumptions.

A product should receive additional investment only when validated evidence demonstrates that continued development creates meaningful customer and business value.

The roadmap connects business strategy with product execution while maintaining clear investment gates.

Product decisions should be based on:

* Customer value
* Business opportunity
* Strategic alignment
* Validated learning
* Business evidence
* Customer feedback
* Measurable outcomes
* Platform growth potential
* Operational sustainability
* Risk
* Available resources

The roadmap does not require every strategic product area to advance at the same speed.

Investment should follow evidence.

# Product Philosophy

EyesightWorks Technologies builds products that solve meaningful business problems.

Technology is a tool for creating customer value, not the reason a product exists.

Products should be built because customers have validated problems, opportunities, or operational needs.

The company prioritizes outcomes over feature quantity.

Every product should contribute to one or more company objectives, including:

* Improving customer productivity
* Reducing operational challenges
* Automating repetitive processes
* Improving business decision-making
* Reducing unnecessary business friction
* Creating sustainable revenue opportunities
* Improving operational efficiency
* Creating reusable company capabilities

The company follows the principle:

**Business → Product → Architecture → Engineering**

The roadmap therefore begins with a business problem rather than a technology preference.

# Product Development Principles

Product decisions should follow the principles below.

---

## Customer Value Before Features

Features should only be developed when they solve meaningful customer problems or support validated business objectives.

The company prioritizes outcomes over feature quantity.

A large feature set does not automatically represent a valuable product.

---

## Validate Before Scaling

Customer demand should be validated before significant engineering investment.

Building large solutions without customer evidence increases unnecessary risk.

The company should seek evidence before committing substantial resources.

---

## MVP First

The company should build the smallest useful product capable of testing the most important business and customer assumptions.

An MVP should create evidence.

It should not attempt to become a complete product before customer validation.

---

## Build Once. Reuse Where Valuable.

Products should reuse shared platform capabilities whenever practical and economically justified.

Common capabilities should become company assets rather than being rebuilt repeatedly.

However, reuse should not create unnecessary abstraction or premature platform complexity.

The decision to extract a capability into a shared platform should be based on:

* Repeated use
* Strategic value
* Cost savings
* Reliability
* Maintainability
* Product demand
* Long-term business value

---

## Depth Before Breadth

EyesightWorks Technologies should focus on making selected products successful before expanding into additional markets.

The company prioritizes:

* Deep customer understanding
* Strong product validation
* Meaningful customer value
* Sustainable operations
* Measurable outcomes

before increasing product breadth.

---

## Evidence Before Expansion

Expansion decisions should be based on validated evidence.

Evidence may include:

* Customer adoption
* Customer retention
* Revenue signals
* Market demand
* Business impact
* Operational performance
* Customer feedback
* Product usage
* Willingness to pay

Expansion should not be based solely on enthusiasm or assumptions.

---

## Simplicity Before Complexity

Products should begin with the simplest solution capable of proving customer value.

Complexity should only increase when additional investment is justified.

Infrastructure, architecture, features, integrations, and operational processes should grow in proportion to validated opportunity.

---

## Controlled Investment

Investment should increase as evidence increases.

Early opportunities should receive controlled investment.

Validated opportunities may receive increased resources.

Products without sufficient evidence should be paused, modified, or stopped rather than continuously funded without justification.

---

## Continuous Learning

Every product stage should generate learning.

Learning should influence:

* Product decisions
* Customer assumptions
* Roadmap priorities
* Business models
* Product requirements
* Architecture decisions
* Investment decisions

The objective is not simply to build products, but to continuously improve the quality of company decisions.

# Product Lifecycle

Every product opportunity should move through a controlled lifecycle.

The lifecycle exists to reduce uncertainty, validate assumptions, control investment, and improve decision quality before additional resources are committed.

The standard lifecycle is:

```text
Business Validation
        ↓
Product Discovery
        ↓
MVP Development
        ↓
Customer Validation
        ↓
Commercialization
        ↓
Growth
        ↓
Platform Integration
        ↓
Maturity
        ↓
Expansion / Optimization / Retirement
```

Not every opportunity must successfully pass through every stage.

A product may be paused, modified, stopped, or returned to an earlier stage when evidence does not support continued progression.

---

# Stage 1 — Business Validation

## Objective

Determine whether the customer problem or business opportunity is meaningful enough to justify further investigation.

## Activities

Typical activities include:

* Customer interviews
* Customer observation
* Market research
* Problem discovery
* Competitive analysis
* Workflow analysis
* Business model evaluation
* Initial willingness-to-pay investigation
* Problem severity assessment
* Target customer identification

## Questions

The company should understand:

* What problem exists?
* Who experiences the problem?
* How frequently does it occur?
* What does the problem currently cost?
* How do customers currently solve it?
* What happens if the problem remains unsolved?
* Is the problem sufficiently important to justify a solution?

## Exit Criteria

Evidence should demonstrate:

* Clearly defined customer problem
* Identified target customer
* Meaningful business impact
* Initial value proposition
* Testable opportunity
* Reasonable evidence that further validation is justified

If the problem cannot be sufficiently validated, the opportunity should not automatically proceed to development.

---

# Stage 2 — Product Discovery

## Objective

Translate the validated business opportunity into a clear product concept.

## Activities

Typical activities include:

* Customer journey definition
* Workflow mapping
* Minimum useful workflow definition
* Value proposition development
* Product assumption identification
* Success metric definition
* Risk identification
* Product requirement discovery
* MVP scope definition

Where a formal Product Requirements Document is required, Document 15 should be used.

## Exit Criteria

The product should have:

* Defined customer
* Defined problem
* Defined value proposition
* Defined minimum useful workflow
* Defined validation objective
* Defined success metrics
* Identified major risks
* Initial product requirements

---

# Stage 3 — MVP Development

## Objective

Build the smallest useful product capable of creating meaningful customer and business evidence.

The MVP should focus on learning rather than completeness.

## Activities

Typical activities include:

* MVP development
* Internal testing
* Initial deployment
* Customer workflow testing
* Basic analytics
* Customer onboarding
* Controlled pilot usage

## Principles

The MVP should:

* Solve the core validated problem
* Contain only necessary functionality
* Be measurable
* Be testable with real users where appropriate
* Avoid unnecessary complexity
* Avoid premature scaling

## Exit Criteria

The MVP should provide:

* Working core functionality
* Ability to test customer assumptions
* Measurable user feedback
* Measurable product outcomes
* Evidence for the next investment decision

---

# Stage 4 — Customer Validation

## Objective

Determine whether customers receive meaningful and measurable value from the product.

## Validation Indicators

Evidence may include:

* Active usage
* Task completion
* Time saved
* Outcome improvement
* Customer feedback
* Customer satisfaction
* Repeated engagement
* Retention
* Willingness to pay
* Revenue signals
* Business impact

Customer validation should demonstrate more than technical functionality.

A product working technically does not automatically mean the product has validated customer value.

## Investment Gate

A product should not automatically enter growth investment without sufficient customer validation evidence.

If evidence is weak, the company may:

* Modify the product
* Narrow the target segment
* Change the value proposition
* Change the workflow
* Return to discovery
* Pause
* Stop

---

## Activities

Commercialization may include:

* Packaging
* Customer onboarding

* Acquisition channels
* Sales process

* Documentation

# Stage 6 — Growth

Improve and expand the product based on validated customer needs and measurable business outcomes.

Growth may include:

* Product improvements
* Performance optimization
* Customer acquisition
* Customer retention improvements
* Revenue expansion
* Operational improvements
* Workflow automation
* Reliability improvements
* Market expansion

Growth should be driven by evidence rather than assumptions.

---

# Stage 7 — Platform Integration

## Objective

Convert successful product capabilities into reusable company capabilities where appropriate.

## Potential Activities

* Creating shared services
* Extracting reusable components
* Creating common workflows
* Establishing internal standards
* Improving platform capabilities
* Documenting reusable capabilities
* Reducing duplication

Not every product must reach platform integration.

Platform decisions should be based on:

* Repeated demand
* Strategic value
* Reuse potential
* Economic value
* Technical sustainability
* Operational requirements

---

# Stage 8 — Maturity

## Objective

Operate the product as a stable, sustainable business capability.

Mature products should demonstrate:

* Stable customer usage
* Reliable operations
* Sustainable economics
* Clear ownership
* Measurable performance
* Established support processes
* Appropriate security
* Operational documentation
* Predictable product governance

Only products with sufficient maturity should normally justify significant expansion.

---

# Stage 9 — Expansion, Optimization, or Retirement

A mature product may enter one of three broad paths.

## Expansion

Expansion may involve:

* New customer segments
* New geographic markets
* New industry applications
* Additional product capabilities
* New distribution channels

Expansion should be supported by evidence.

## Optimization

Optimization may focus on:

* Improving efficiency
* Reducing operating costs
* Improving customer outcomes
* Improving retention
* Improving reliability
* Simplifying the product
* Improving unit economics

## Retirement

A product may be paused or retired when:

* Validated evidence remains weak
* Customer value is insufficient
* Retention remains poor
* Economics are unsustainable
* Risk becomes unacceptable
* Operational burden becomes excessive
* Strategic opportunity cost exceeds expected value
* The underlying customer problem is no longer sufficiently important

Retirement decisions should be documented through the Decision Log.

# MVP Strategy

An MVP exists to answer a business question.

The purpose of an MVP is not to create a complete product.

The purpose is to reduce uncertainty through validated learning.

Examples of MVP questions include:

* Does this customer problem exist?
* Is the problem sufficiently important?
* Will customers use this solution?
* Will customers pay for this value?
* Which customer segment benefits most?
* Which workflow creates the highest customer value?
* Which features are actually necessary?
* Can the business deliver the expected value sustainably?

MVP success is measured by learning quality and validated outcomes, not feature quantity.

# Product Validation Framework

Before significant product investment, every opportunity should define:

1. Business Hypothesis
2. Target Customer
3. Validation Method
4. Success Criteria
5. Failure Criteria
6. Decision

---

# Business Hypothesis

What customer problem do we believe exists?

The hypothesis should clearly describe:

* Target customer
* Customer problem
* Expected value
* Expected business impact

A hypothesis should be testable.

---

# Target Customer

Who specifically experiences the problem?

The company should identify:

* Customer segment
* Business type
* User profile
* Customer workflow
* Buying role
* Buying decision process
* Adoption conditions

The company should avoid broad definitions such as:

> "Everyone is our customer."

A clear target customer improves validation quality and acquisition focus.

---

# Validation Method

How will the assumption be tested?

Possible methods include:

* Customer interviews
* Customer observation
* Workflow analysis
* Prototype testing
* MVP usage analysis
* Market experiments
* Landing pages
* Demos
* Pilot engagements
* Concierge services
* Manual processes
* Revenue testing

Each validation experiment should define:

* Hypothesis
* Evidence required
* Success signal
* Failure signal
* Decision that follows

---

# Success Criteria

What measurable evidence confirms progress?

Examples include:

* Customer adoption
* User engagement
* Task completion
* Time saved
* Positive customer feedback
* Customer retention
* Revenue signals
* Willingness to pay
* Business impact
* Operational improvement

Success criteria should be defined before the validation activity where practical.

---

# Failure Criteria

What evidence indicates the assumption is incorrect or insufficiently supported?

Examples include:

* Lack of customer interest
* Low engagement
* No willingness to pay
* Low retention
* Unclear customer value
* Poor business impact
* Excessive acquisition difficulty
* Unsustainable operating requirements

Failure evidence should not be treated as wasted effort.

It should improve company learning and future decisions.

---

# Decision

Based on evidence, the outcome should be one of:

* Continue
* Modify
* Pause
* Stop

Where appropriate, an opportunity may return to an earlier stage of the product lifecycle.

All major decisions should be recorded through the Decision Log.

---

# Beachhead Market Strategy

Every product should begin with a clearly defined initial customer segment.

The company should avoid broad positioning such as:

> "Everyone is our customer."

A successful product requires focus.

Each product should define:

* Specific customer group
* Specific business problem
* Specific value proposition
* Specific workflow
* Specific acquisition approach
* Specific validation criteria

The initial market should be narrow enough to allow meaningful learning.

Expansion should occur only after sufficient success has been demonstrated within the initial market.

---

# Roadmap Views

The roadmap should maintain four strategic views:

* **Now**
* **Next**
* **Later**
* **Parking Lot**

## Now

Work currently receiving active attention or investment.

## Next

Validated opportunities likely to receive attention after current priorities.

## Later

Potential opportunities that require further evidence or depend on future conditions.

## Parking Lot

Ideas and opportunities that should be preserved without active investment.

The Parking Lot prevents potentially valuable ideas from being forgotten while protecting the company from premature execution.

# Decision Principles

Product decisions should improve one or more of the following:

* Customer value
* Business sustainability
* Strategic alignment
* Learning speed
* Platform growth
* Operational efficiency
* Long-term company capability

Products should not continue indefinitely when evidence does not justify further investment.

Complexity, investment, and expansion should always remain proportional to validated opportunity.

The company should prefer evidence over enthusiasm.

---

# Stop and Retirement Criteria

A product or product initiative should be considered for pause, modification, or retirement when:

* The customer problem cannot be sufficiently validated
* Customer value remains unclear
* Customer adoption remains insufficient
* Retention remains poor
* Customers demonstrate insufficient willingness to pay
* Economics remain unsustainable
* Operational complexity becomes excessive
* Risk becomes unacceptable
* Strategic opportunity cost becomes too high
* The product no longer aligns with company strategy

The decision should consider the total evidence rather than a single metric.

Retirement decisions should be documented through the Decision Log.
