# Product Roadmap

## Executive Summary

This document defines the strategic product roadmap for EyesightWorks Technologies.

The purpose of this document is to establish how the company identifies, evaluates, prioritizes, validates, and invests in product opportunities.

The roadmap is designed to guide strategic decisions rather than predict delivery schedules.

Product decisions should be based on:

- Customer value
- Business opportunity
- Strategic alignment
- Validated learning
- Platform growth potential

Product development should follow evidence rather than assumptions.

A product should receive additional investment only when validated evidence demonstrates that continued development creates meaningful customer and business value.

# Product Philosophy

EyesightWorks Technologies builds products that solve meaningful business problems.

Technology is a tool for creating customer value, not the reason a product exists.

Every product should contribute to one or more company objectives:

- Improve customer productivity
- Reduce operational challenges
- Automate repetitive processes
- Improve business decision-making
- Create sustainable revenue opportunities

Products should be built because customers have validated problems, not because technologies are interesting.

The company follows the principle:

**Business → Product → Architecture → Engineering**

Business needs define products.

Products define architecture requirements.

Architecture guides engineering decisions.

---

# Product Development Principles

Product decisions should follow these principles.

---

## Customer Value Before Features

Features should only be developed when they solve meaningful customer problems.

The company prioritizes outcomes over feature quantity.

---

## Validate Before Scaling

Customer demand should be validated before significant engineering investment.

Building large solutions without customer evidence increases unnecessary risk.

---

## Build Once. Reuse Everywhere.

Products should reuse shared platform capabilities whenever practical.

Common capabilities should become company assets rather than being rebuilt repeatedly.

---

## Depth Before Breadth

EyesightWorks Technologies will focus on making selected products successful before expanding into additional markets.

The company prioritizes:

- Deep customer understanding
- Strong product-market fit
- Sustainable operations

before increasing product breadth.

---

## Evidence Before Expansion

Expansion decisions should be based on validated evidence.

Evidence may include:

- Customer adoption
- Revenue signals
- Market demand
- Operational performance

---

## Simplicity Before Complexity

Products should begin with the simplest solution capable of proving customer value.

Complexity should only increase when additional investment is justified.

# Business Value Score

Before significant engineering investment begins, every product opportunity should complete a Business Value Score evaluation.

The Business Value Score provides a consistent framework for comparing product opportunities using measurable business criteria.

The evaluation should consider:

- Customer problem importance
- Market opportunity
- Revenue potential
- Strategic alignment
- Platform reuse potential
- Engineering effort
- Operational complexity
- Business risk

The complete evaluation process is defined in:

**12-business-value-score.md**

Products should proceed based on validated evidence rather than assumptions.

A high score does not automatically guarantee approval.

The final decision should consider strategic priorities, available resources, and company objectives.

# Product Investment Stages

Every product should progress through investment stages based on evidence.

---

# Stage 1 — Business Validation

## Objective

Determine whether the customer problem is worth solving.

## Activities

Typical activities include:

- Customer interviews
- Market research
- Problem validation
- Competitive analysis
- Business model evaluation

## Success Criteria

Evidence should demonstrate:

- Clearly defined customer problem
- Identified target customer
- Initial value proposition
- Reasonable market opportunity

---

# Stage 2 — MVP Development

## Objective

Build the smallest solution capable of testing customer value.

The MVP should focus on learning rather than building a complete product.

## Activities

Typical activities include:

- MVP development
- Internal testing
- Initial deployment
- Customer workflow testing

## Success Criteria

The MVP should provide:

- Working core functionality
- Ability to test customer assumptions
- Measurable user feedback

---

# Stage 3 — Customer Validation

## Objective

Determine whether customers receive measurable value from the product.

## Validation Indicators

Evidence may include:

- Active usage
- Customer feedback
- Customer retention
- Repeated engagement
- Willingness to pay
- Business impact

A product should not enter growth investment without customer validation evidence.

---

# Stage 4 — Growth

## Objective

Improve and expand the product based on validated customer needs.

## Activities

Growth activities may include:

- Product improvements
- Performance optimization
- Customer acquisition
- Customer retention improvements
- Revenue expansion

Growth should be driven by customer evidence rather than assumptions.

---

# Stage 5 — Platform Integration

## Objective

Convert successful product capabilities into reusable company platform capabilities where appropriate.

Potential activities include:

- Creating shared services
- Extracting reusable components
- Improving internal standards
- Documenting capabilities

Not every product must reach platform integration.

Platform decisions should be based on strategic value and evidence.

---

# MVP Strategy

An MVP exists to answer a business question.

The purpose of an MVP is not to create a complete product.

The purpose is to reduce uncertainty through validated learning.

Examples of MVP questions:

- Does this customer problem exist?
- Will customers use this solution?
- Will customers pay for this value?
- Which customer segment benefits most?
- Which features create the highest customer value?

MVP success is measured by learning quality, not feature quantity.

---

# Product Validation Framework

Before significant product investment, every opportunity should define the following.

---

# Business Hypothesis

What customer problem do we believe exists?

The hypothesis should clearly describe:

- Target customer
- Customer problem
- Expected value

---

# Target Customer

Who specifically experiences this problem?

The company should identify:

- Customer segment
- Business type
- User profile
- Buying decision process

---

# Validation Method

How will the assumption be tested?

Possible methods include:

- Customer interviews
- Prototype testing
- MVP usage analysis
- Market experiments
- Revenue testing

---

# Success Criteria

What measurable evidence confirms progress?

Examples:

- Customer adoption
- User engagement
- Revenue signals
- Positive feedback
- Retention

---

# Failure Criteria

What evidence indicates the assumption is incorrect?

Examples:

- Lack of customer interest
- Low engagement
- No willingness to pay
- Unclear customer value

---

# Decision

Based on evidence, the outcome should be one of:

- Continue
- Modify
- Pause
- Stop

All major decisions should be recorded through the Decision Log.

---

# Beachhead Market Strategy

Every product should begin with a clearly defined initial customer segment.

The company should avoid broad positioning such as:

> "Everyone is our customer."

A successful product requires focus.

Each product should define:

- Specific customer group
- Specific business problem
- Specific value proposition
- Specific acquisition approach

Expansion should occur only after success has been demonstrated within the initial market.

---

# Roadmap Governance

The product roadmap is a living strategic document.

It should be reviewed when:

- Customer evidence changes
- Market conditions change
- Business priorities change
- Product assumptions fail
- Strategic opportunities emerge

Roadmap decisions should be supported by evidence.

Significant changes should be documented through the Decision Log.

The roadmap should guide investment decisions while remaining flexible as new information becomes available.
